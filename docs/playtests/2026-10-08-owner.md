## First World

- Fairly basic I guess
- Drills feel like a tutorial, but that's probably fine
- Challenges don't feel very challenging
  - It also doesn't feel like they really combine the moves yet because there's often ones that could come straight out of the drill for the corresponding move. 
  - Could be a bit faster / have shorter timeouts
- I think this world could already use counts because you otherwise train repeated key presses, which is not a habit you should build. Or if not it should be the first level of the second world and not the last.

## Second World

- Overall similar problems as the first world: Repeated keystrokes and repeat of raw concepts without combinations in challenges
- 2.3 contained the first move I had forgotten (^)
- 2.4 was the first level where I failed an item in a challenge because I was too slow and thought about the way with the minimal keystrokes with counts

## Third world

- 3.1 was the first level where I learned something new. It also was the first level where I failed to advance in the challenge because I had too few solved items.
- Combinations of concepts really start to work here because d plus the motions up until now just makes sense to combine. This might also serve as a good template: The challenge for a thing should combine it with what you have until now. It should not leave out the thing you just learned.
- 3.2 was super easy in comparison
- 3.3 first one where I thought "yeah, that's a useful move to use everyday". However, the challenge used lots of unrelated items again.

## Fourth world

- 4.1 funnily the opposite of 3.3: "oh yeah, there's f. Let's use it for everything now". Cost me some points in the challenge, but was a viable alternative.
- 4.2 "woohoo, so many movement options :) hard to decide what to do" was my thinking during this challenge
- 4.3 Finally some combined moves. That don't show up in the challenge, though.

## General remarks

- In general, I feel like the challenges just repeat moves you learned in the past without context. This isn't really applying what you learned, this is just mindlessly repeating katas.
- I liked how 4.3 combined moves, but I feel like that should have been part of every level until then. Of course some moves can't be properly combinded (f.e. x, d and c). But when you teach something new, you should explain what it combines with. Maybe that would be good as a second part of the drill.
- Also, every item in a challenge for a level should contain the move learned in that level. It just doesn't make sense to do otherwise. On the other hand, just doing the same move over and over would be boring, so it should contain some combined moves. Not exclusively, it could become overwhelming otherwise. But they should be there. Maybe that could be part of a parameter for future higher difficulties for the challenge.
- All worlds could use a "Boss" level at the end that is a bit harder / has more entries. Just to introduce the concept that you need to beat a boss to advance to the next level. Also, it would be interesting to combine not only two but several concepts that you need to chain as a sequence of moves for the final items of the boss level. In that case, we should track which concepts are included, because this is one way to increase the difficulty and it would be interesting to evaluate where you fail and what your weakest move is.
- Also a boss level would be a good place to put incompatible concepts into the same level. That selection should probably still mandate concepts from the world the boss is for.
- I didn't try out habit mode yet, nothing led to it. Feels a bit like a gimmick. But it could be enabled by default in challenges once you learn to count.
